# Wedding Table Nine

**Level 2 — Maintain knowledge, belief and canon**

**Genre:** ensemble comedy / farce

A last-minute seating reshuffle puts six wedding guests together whose relationships, secrets and assumptions are known differently by everyone at the table.

## Prompt

```text
Use Narrative Production Skills to develop a 3,000-word ensemble comedy titled “Wedding Table Nine”, with explicit continuity tracking.

Premise:
A last-minute seating-plan disaster places six guests at the same wedding table: the bride's former flatmate Amira, her ex Ben, Ben's current partner Chloe, the bride's cousin Daniel, family friend Ruth, and a stranger named Leo who has been mistaken for somebody else. Each knows a different subset of the relationships at the table, and several are actively concealing why they came.

Requirements:
- Genre: ensemble comedy / farce
- Tone: fast, socially observant, warm rather than cruel
- Keep the comedy driven by asymmetric knowledge, mistaken assumptions and character choices
- Track who knows each relationship fact, who merely suspects it, and who is wrong
- At least two misunderstandings should be resolved while creating a new problem
- Avoid making everyone behave irrationally just to preserve the joke
- The final resolution should change at least two relationships without requiring a melodramatic reveal

Deliverables:
- concise character profiles for the six table guests
- continuity record distinguishing canonical facts, beliefs, secrets and mistaken assumptions
- scene-level outline
- complete story draft
- continuity-aware evaluation and targeted revision plan

Workflow:
- Establish the objective relationship graph first
- Record each character's knowledge separately
- Outline comic escalations before writing dialogue
- Assemble only task-relevant continuity context for each scene
- Evaluate both humour and information-state correctness
- Fix the smallest scene or line range responsible for any knowledge leak or broken setup

What to optimise for:
- distinct ensemble voices
- information-state discipline
- escalating comic consequences
- earned reversals
- readable relationship complexity
- continuity that remains invisible to the reader
```

## What this example demonstrates

Add explicit continuity because relationships, promises, identity and evidence now depend on who knows, believes, conceals or misinterprets what.

```text
objective state + beliefs + secrets + uncertainty → task context → scenes → continuity evaluation → local correction
```

This example is one of three productions at Level 2. The level is the capability target; the different stories test whether that capability transfers across genres, tones and audience expectations.
