# Returns Desk

**Level 3 — Develop and execute a screenplay**

**Genre:** workplace fantasy comedy

A department-store returns clerk is asked to refund a dragon egg, but every normal policy choice creates a new practical problem as the egg begins to hatch.

## Prompt

```text
Use Narrative Production Skills to develop and write a 6–8 page screenplay scene titled “Returns Desk”.

Premise:
At a very ordinary department-store returns desk, clerk Nia is asked by an equally ordinary customer to return a large warm egg bought from the seasonal homeware department. The receipt lists it as a decorative stone. During the conversation, the egg begins showing increasingly specific signs that something alive is inside it.

Requirements:
- Medium: screenplay
- Genre: workplace fantasy comedy
- Tone: dry, escalating, grounded performance inside an absurd situation
- Primary objective: Nia wants to resolve the return without breaking policy or creating a scene
- Customer objective: obtain a refund before the egg hatches
- Give the scene a clear entry state, escalation, turn and changed exit state
- Use physical action as part of the comedy, not dialogue alone
- Do not specify lenses, camera moves, lighting setups or shot composition
- Output screenplay material in Fountain-compatible form

Workflow:
- Develop the scene objective, conflict and minimal fantasy rule
- Create a scene card before writing pages
- Identify the major comic beats and escalation points
- Draft the screenplay from the scene card
- Evaluate dialogue rhythm, action clarity, scene turn and medium fit
- Create a bounded revision plan and rewrite only the parts responsible for weak timing or unclear action

What to optimise for:
- screenplay economy
- comic timing
- playable physical action
- distinct voices
- escalation from policy language to genuine consequence
- a strong final beat
```

## What this example demonstrates

Translate medium-independent story decisions into scene cards and Fountain-compatible screenplay pages across comedy, romance and action while keeping audiovisual direction downstream.

```text
story intent → scene cards → screenplay → developmental evaluation → bounded screenplay revision
```

This example is one of three productions at Level 3. The level is the capability target; the different stories test whether that capability transfers across genres, tones and audience expectations.
