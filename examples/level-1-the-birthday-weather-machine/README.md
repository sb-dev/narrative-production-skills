# The Birthday Weather Machine

**Level 1 — Develop and revise one complete story**

**Genre:** family science-fiction comedy

An eleven-year-old inventor tries to save her little brother's outdoor birthday from rain and discovers that controlling one tiny patch of weather is harder than building the machine.

## Prompt

```text
Use Narrative Production Skills to develop a 2,000-word family science-fiction comedy titled “The Birthday Weather Machine”.

Premise:
Eleven-year-old Priya Shah has promised her younger brother a perfect garden birthday. When the forecast turns to heavy rain, she secretly activates the homemade weather machine she has been building for a school project. It works, but every correction creates a new hyper-local weather problem somewhere else in the party.

Requirements:
- Genre: family science-fiction comedy
- Audience: children / family
- Tone: energetic, warm, funny, never cynical
- Point of view: close third person through Priya
- Keep the science playful and internally consistent rather than technically explanatory
- Escalation must come from Priya's attempts to fix earlier consequences
- Give the younger brother an active role rather than making him only the reason for the plot
- The resolution should require Priya to accept imperfection, not simply invent a stronger machine
- Avoid humiliation-based humour

Workflow:
- Explore three different escalation patterns before selecting one
- Define the machine's minimal story rules and preserve them
- Build a compact causal outline with increasingly difficult consequences
- Draft the story from the selected outline
- Evaluate rule consistency, comic timing, age-appropriate voice and emotional resolution
- Revise the smallest sufficient parts without changing the approved machine rules

What to optimise for:
- clear cause and effect
- escalating visualisable comedy expressed through prose
- sibling characterisation
- family-friendly stakes
- payoff of earlier setup
- a satisfying emotional choice
```

## What this example demonstrates

Prove the complete narrative-production loop on compact short fiction across very different tones before continuity infrastructure or cross-domain handoffs are needed.

```text
brief → concept alternatives → selection → outline → draft → evaluation → revision plan → targeted revision
```

This example is one of three productions at Level 1. The level is the capability target; the different stories test whether that capability transfers across genres, tones and audience expectations.
