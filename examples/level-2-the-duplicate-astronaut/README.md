# The Duplicate Astronaut

**Level 2 — Maintain knowledge, belief and canon**

**Genre:** science fiction / identity story

Two astronauts return from the same one-person mission with identical memories up to a short communications blackout, and both sincerely believe they are the original.

## Prompt

```text
Use Narrative Production Skills to develop a 3,000-word science-fiction story titled “The Duplicate Astronaut”, with rigorous continuity and uncertainty tracking.

Premise:
A one-person research capsule returns with two versions of astronaut Dr Mara Ilyan. Both have identical memories up to an eleven-minute communications blackout near the mission's end. After that point their memories diverge. Ground control possesses incomplete telemetry that confirms some events, contradicts others, and leaves the central identity question unresolved.

Requirements:
- Genre: science fiction / identity story
- Tone: intelligent, humane, restrained
- Do not declare one Mara fake unless the story has earned objective evidence for it
- Treat identity claims, memories, telemetry, character beliefs and reader hypotheses as different information categories
- Avoid technobabble as a substitute for causality
- Both Maras must remain active characters with meaningful choices
- The ending should resolve an immediate human problem even if the metaphysical question remains uncertain

Deliverables:
- minimal mission timeline
- character profiles
- continuity record separating canonical telemetry, memories, beliefs, uncertainty and secrets
- story outline and draft
- continuity evaluation identifying any accidental conversion of uncertainty into fact

Workflow:
- Establish what is objectively known before exploring explanations
- Track each Mara's divergent post-blackout knowledge independently
- Keep speculative explanations as candidates until established
- Assemble scene context from only the evidence available at that point
- Evaluate continuity and identity-state discipline before stylistic polish
- Revise only affected material if a scene asserts more certainty than the evidence permits

What to optimise for:
- disciplined uncertainty
- character empathy
- identity continuity
- evidence-aware causality
- thematic depth without exposition overload
- an ending that is emotionally decisive but epistemically honest
```

## What this example demonstrates

Add explicit continuity because relationships, promises, identity and evidence now depend on who knows, believes, conceals or misinterprets what.

```text
objective state + beliefs + secrets + uncertainty → task context → scenes → continuity evaluation → local correction
```

This example is one of three productions at Level 2. The level is the capability target; the different stories test whether that capability transfers across genres, tones and audience expectations.
