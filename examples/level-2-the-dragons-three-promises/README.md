# The Dragon's Three Promises

**Level 2 — Maintain knowledge, belief and canon**

**Genre:** fantasy

A dragon has separately made three binding promises to three people, and a single midsummer festival makes it impossible to honour all three literally.

## Prompt

```text
Use Narrative Production Skills to develop a 3,000-word fantasy story titled “The Dragon's Three Promises”, with explicit world and continuity state.

Premise:
Orm, an old valley dragon, has made three binding promises over many years: never allow fire near beekeeper Mara's hives; light the royal forge for Princess Elian on midsummer night; and protect blacksmith Tomas's family whenever the festival bells ring. None of the three people knows the full set of promises. This midsummer, all three obligations collide.

Requirements:
- Genre: character-driven fantasy
- Tone: humane, wry, mythic without archaic imitation
- The three promises are genuine world constraints, not suggestions that can be ignored
- Track exactly who knows which promise and when they learn the others
- Do not solve the conflict through an undisclosed fourth magical power
- The resolution may reinterpret wording only if the relevant wording was established earlier
- Keep worldbuilding limited to what the promise conflict requires

Deliverables:
- minimal world-bible entry for binding dragon promises
- character profiles for Orm, Mara, Elian and Tomas
- continuity record for promises, knowledge and planned revelations
- outline and complete story draft
- evaluation focused on rule consistency and fair setup/payoff

Workflow:
- Define the promise rules before plotting the resolution
- Separate canonical promise wording from character interpretations
- Track knowledge changes scene by scene
- Draft from the approved rules and selected outline
- Evaluate for accidental rule-breaking and hidden-information leakage
- Revise locally without changing established promise wording to rescue the ending

What to optimise for:
- internally consistent fantasy rules
- competing legitimate obligations
- character-specific interpretations
- fair resolution
- minimal lore
- emotional consequence of the solution
```

## What this example demonstrates

Add explicit continuity because relationships, promises, identity and evidence now depend on who knows, believes, conceals or misinterprets what.

```text
objective state + beliefs + secrets + uncertainty → task context → scenes → continuity evaluation → local correction
```

This example is one of three productions at Level 2. The level is the capability target; the different stories test whether that capability transfers across genres, tones and audience expectations.
