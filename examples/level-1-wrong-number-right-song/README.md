# Wrong Number, Right Song

**Level 1 — Develop and revise one complete story**

**Genre:** romantic comedy

A struggling songwriter receives a voice note meant for someone else after a disastrous blind date, and a polite correction turns into an unexpectedly honest conversation over one evening.

## Prompt

```text
Use Narrative Production Skills to develop a 1,800–2,200 word romantic-comedy short story titled “Wrong Number, Right Song”.

Premise:
At the end of a bad week, songwriter Eli Mercer receives a voice note from Maya Chen describing a disastrous blind date. She sent it to the wrong number. Eli replies only to tell her, but their brief exchange becomes a funny, increasingly candid conversation while he is trying to finish a song for an open-mic set that night.

Requirements:
- Genre: romantic comedy
- Tone: warm, witty, contemporary, emotionally grounded
- Point of view: close third person through Eli
- Time span: one evening
- Keep the cast small
- The relationship should develop through specific choices and conversation, not instant destiny
- The song may reflect the evening but must not become a grand romantic gesture that resolves everything
- Avoid manipulative misunderstandings that could be solved by one obvious sentence
- End on a credible opening rather than a guaranteed happily-ever-after

Workflow:
- Generate three materially different ways the mistaken message could develop into a complete story
- Select one based on character chemistry, comic escalation and emotional credibility
- Define only the character details needed to make the dialogue distinctive
- Outline the complete story before drafting prose
- Draft with particular attention to dialogue rhythm and subtext
- Evaluate humour, character agency, pacing and emotional payoff
- Revise only the scenes or passages responsible for weak chemistry or forced comedy

What to optimise for:
- distinctive character voices
- earned chemistry
- comic escalation without farce
- emotional movement beneath the jokes
- economical structure
- an ending that feels specific rather than generic
```

## What this example demonstrates

Prove the complete narrative-production loop on compact short fiction across very different tones before continuity infrastructure or cross-domain handoffs are needed.

```text
brief → concept alternatives → selection → outline → draft → evaluation → revision plan → targeted revision
```

This example is one of three productions at Level 1. The level is the capability target; the different stories test whether that capability transfers across genres, tones and audience expectations.
