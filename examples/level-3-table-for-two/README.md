# Table for Two

**Level 3 — Develop and execute a screenplay**

**Genre:** romantic comedy

Two people at neighbouring restaurant tables realise their blind dates have been accidentally swapped, but neither wants to interrupt the unexpectedly better conversation.

## Prompt

```text
Use Narrative Production Skills to develop and write a 6–8 page romantic-comedy screenplay scene titled “Table for Two”.

Premise:
At a crowded restaurant, Aya and Marcus gradually realise that a booking mix-up has seated each of them with the other person's blind date. Their actual dates are at the next table and have not noticed. Aya and Marcus now have to decide whether to correct the mistake just as their own conversation starts becoming the best part of either evening.

Requirements:
- Medium: screenplay
- Genre: romantic comedy
- Tone: observant, warm, quick without becoming sitcom-broad
- Build chemistry through specific behaviour, listening and subtext rather than declarations
- Give both leads active objectives unrelated to simply “find love”
- The neighbouring dates should create pressure without becoming caricatures
- The scene must contain a meaningful turn that changes what Aya and Marcus are willing to risk
- Do not dictate cinematography or visual production choices
- Output Fountain-compatible screenplay pages

Workflow:
- Develop a scene card with entry state, objectives, conflict, beats, turn and exit state
- Explore at least two possible scene turns before selecting one
- Draft the scene with attention to interruptions, subtext and comic timing
- Evaluate chemistry, character agency, dialogue economy and scene progression
- Revise the smallest affected exchange rather than rewriting the entire scene

What to optimise for:
- earned chemistry
- subtext
- comic tension
- balanced character agency
- clean scene architecture
- a final beat that opens possibility without over-resolving the relationship
```

## What this example demonstrates

Translate medium-independent story decisions into scene cards and Fountain-compatible screenplay pages across comedy, romance and action while keeping audiovisual direction downstream.

```text
story intent → scene cards → screenplay → developmental evaluation → bounded screenplay revision
```

This example is one of three productions at Level 3. The level is the capability target; the different stories test whether that capability transfers across genres, tones and audience expectations.
