# Mystery Continuity

**Level 2 — Maintain knowledge, belief and canon across scenes**

Three witnesses remember the same museum theft differently, and one of them is telling the truth for the wrong reason.

## Prompt

```text
Use Narrative Production Skills to develop a contained mystery in which three witnesses remember the same museum theft differently.

Premise:
Three witnesses give conflicting accounts of a museum theft. One of them is telling the truth about what happened, but for a reason that makes their testimony misleading in a different way.

Requirements:
- Genre: contemporary mystery
- Tone: controlled, intelligent, suspicious without becoming melodramatic
- Use conflicting beliefs rather than simple factual contradictions
- Keep the thief's identity unresolved during the early scenes
- One witness must know something they do not disclose
- One established camera outage must be canonical before the theft
- A later alarm-sequence reveal may be planned, but must not be treated as canonical before it appears in the story

Workflow:
- Develop the minimum character and event material needed for the mystery
- Record objective truth separately from each character's beliefs, knowledge and secrets
- Mark future reveals as planned rather than canonical
- Assemble only the continuity context needed for the next scene
- Draft the next scene without leaking information a character does not know
- Evaluate the scene for continuity, knowledge-state and mystery leakage
- If a defect is found, identify the smallest owning artifact and revise only the affected material

What to optimise for:
- belief-state consistency
- information control
- fair but incomplete clues
- planned versus canonical separation
- character-specific knowledge
- no accidental reveal leakage
```

## What this example demonstrates

Continuity appears because the story requires it, not because every narrative project starts with a database or graph.

The key distinction is:

```text
objective truth
≠ character belief
≠ character knowledge
≠ secret
≠ planned future reveal
≠ canonical fact
```
