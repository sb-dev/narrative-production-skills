# Narrative Write

Turn sufficiently developed narrative intent into actual prose or screenplay execution.

## Operating sequence

1. Identify form and target scope: scene, chapter, sequence, episode, manuscript section, or screenplay section.
2. Load only relevant story artifacts and nearby narrative.
3. Determine `draft`, `refine`, or `final`.
4. Preserve approved upstream decisions.
5. Write at the requested resolution.
6. Surface discoveries that materially change upstream story state.

## Context

Typical context includes the current scene card, relevant beats, involved character profiles, applicable world constraints, current continuity, unresolved setup/payoff, nearby narrative and explicit voice requirements. Do not load the whole project automatically.

## Rough writing

Prioritise scene function, behaviour, conflict, information flow, meaningful change and continuity before line polish.

## Refinement

Improve only the requested dimensions: voice, dialogue, subtext, pacing, specificity, description, POV, rhythm or screenplay economy. Preserve declared outcomes, reveals and other approved decisions.

## Prose

Support narration, interiority, POV, dialogue, chapters and scene transitions without forcing screenplay constraints.

## Screenplay

Support scene headings, visible action and dialogue. Prefer Fountain when a portable screenplay text format is useful. Do not invent shot lists, cinematography, storyboards or visual character design unless handed to the downstream domain.

## Discovery writing

If drafting reveals a material story discovery, identify the affected planning artifact and update or recommend updating it. Do not silently diverge.

## Upstream conflicts

If the requested execution cannot satisfy an approved upstream decision, surface the conflict instead of hiding it with prose.
